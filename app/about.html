Medicare Fraud Flagger

About this score

A high score is a resemblance, not a fraud finding.

What the number means

A high score is a resemblance, not a fraud finding. The model compares a billing row with providers who were later excluded. It does not decide that the provider committed fraud.

Training

The second forest was fit on 2013 through 2022. 2023 was the selection year. 2024 was held out. The earlier training window has 1270 fraud provider-year rows. An earlier shallow model placed 11/48 of the 2024 fraud rows in the top 1% and 18/48 in the top 5%. The saved blend places 26/48 in the top 1% and 26/48 in the top 5%. Those 48 fraud rows are below the published external-validation floor of at least 100 events, ideally 200 or more, so those two shares are not a precise estimate of next-year capture.

For the demo

App name: Medicare Fraud Flagger. Purpose: look up a provider's billing row and see whether that row resembles providers who were later excluded. Audience: a congressional office, a student, or a reporter who wants the resemblance without treating it as a finding. Tools and languages: Python, HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. The score comes from a saved blend of two forests. The map uses Leaflet.

Show the app working in this order. Search Prince Laboratories, NPI 1124725817, for 2024. Open Reliable Results Labs, NPI 1639879034, for the same year. Click a map pin and show that it opens the same row. Type a billing row with a blank feature and show the incomplete mark. Read the resemblance line on the result.

Written answers

Title: Medicare Fraud Flagger.

Purpose: a person can look up a Medicare provider by name, city, or NPI, or type a new billing row, and see a percentile. A high score means the billing profile resembles providers later excluded for a fraud-type reason. The page says that on every result.

Inspiration: published work by Bauder and Khoshgoftaar scored Medicare billing rows against later exclusions. I wanted that kind of lookup on one screen, with the two lab names a judge can open in a minute, and with the score kept separate from a finding.

Technical difficulty: the rolled provider-year table is too large for a static JSON file, and a typed row still has to score against the saved blend. I kept the search table and the model on a local Python server. The page asks that server for a stored percentile or a live score. A blank feature stays blank and the row is marked incomplete.

What I learned: a 2024 top 1 percent count of 26 of 48 looks strong until you put it next to the published floor of at least 100 events. The share is a resemblance on a small holdout, so the page prints the count and the caveat together. My biggest takeaway is that the disclaimer has to sit on the result, not only on this page.

A 2.0 would add billing years before 2018, a public URL for the contest window, and a second model only after the first page already runs. It would also put the student's own name in the demo once that name is known. This draft leaves the name blank on purpose.

Sources and tools

  • Grok, in the Grok Build CLI, was used to build the pipeline, the model step, and this page. The student describes their own part in the portal, in their own words.
  • CMS files: Medicare Physician & Other Practitioners by Provider and Service, 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, and 2024, from data.cms.gov. Each year is rolled to one row per rendering NPI.
  • LEIE: the HHS OIG List of Excluded Individuals/Entities, the September 2026 UPDATED.csv. A provider-year is fraud only for exclusion types 1128a1, 1128a3, 1128b6, and 1128b7 when the exclusion year is after the billing year.
  • Saved blend: models/candidates/blend_357_247.joblib. The score is the higher of two saved ranks. Each rank is a page forest plus a second RandomForestClassifier fit on 2013 through 2022. The local Python server loads that file and scores a typed row. A static file server cannot score a row.
  • Map library: Leaflet. A pin uses the row's city, state, and ZIP. Clicking the pin opens that same row.
  • Page layout adapted from ProPublica Prescriber Checkup, designed by the ProPublica news apps team including Lena Groeger, Jennifer LaFleur, Charles Ornstein, and Tracy Weber. Their stylesheet and branding are not used. The layout source is the June 2019 public app at projects.propublica.org/checkup.

Missing fields stay missing. The row is marked incomplete. Name, NPI, city, state, and ZIP identify the row and place the pin. They are not features.
