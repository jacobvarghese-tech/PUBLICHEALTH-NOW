# Congressional App Challenge — agent reference

Last verified: 2026-10-15, from official pages and the 2026 rules PDF.

This file is the working brief for building and submitting an app for the **2026 Congressional App Challenge**. Prefer this file over the students landing page, which still shows the finished 2025 dates.

## What it is

A free, district-by-district coding contest for U.S. middle and high school students. Each participating U.S. House member hosts a local competition. Students build an original app and compete in one district only. There is no national assigned topic and no required language or platform.

## Eligibility

Age: enrolled in middle school or high school on October 26, 2026. Middle school usually means grades 6–8 and varies by region. Students who graduate before the deadline are ineligible.

Residency: U.S. resident at submission. No citizenship requirement.

District: may compete only in a district that is hosting, and only in one district. That district is either where the student lives or where the student attends school. Cannot enter two districts.

Teams: individual, or up to 4 students. Five or more is ineligible. At least half the team must live or attend school in the same district being entered (1 of 2, 2 of 3, or 2 of 4). One team participates once as a group.

One entry per person per year. A student cannot submit more than one app, including across teams.

Prior work: any app created after October 30, 2025. A 2.0 of an older app is allowed only if the new coding is what gets highlighted.

District list was last updated on the site on 2026-10-05. Most House districts, D.C., Puerto Rico, Guam, and the Northern Mariana Islands are hosting. Some are blank. If the home district is not hosting, the school district can be used only if the student actually attends school there and that district is hosting. Do not count on a member signing up in the last 11 days.

## App rules

Topic: any topic. A member’s office may set a local theme. Check that office before locking a concept if a local theme is possible.

Platform: any, including web, desktop/PC, mobile, browser extensions, robots, AR, VR, wearables, smart devices, and embedded systems.

Language: any, including C, C++, Objective-C, C#, Java, JavaScript, Python, Ruby, PHP, Swift, and block-based coding. Beginners are expected. In 2025, over 30% of participants described themselves as beginners.

Functionality: the app must demonstrate some degree of functionality. Judges may request access to the app and source code in person or by any reasonable method. Refusing that request disqualifies the entry.

Originality: original work, coded and technically developed by the student or team. Open-source libraries, frameworks, and external tools are allowed if documented, and the project must still show significant personal effort and technical understanding. No other party should have rights or interest in the app.

AI: allowed if fully disclosed in the submission, used only to support specific parts, and not the entirety of the technical development. The student must still show significant individual contribution and technical understanding.

Content: must be decent and respectful. Prohibited: indecency, defamation, obvious bad taste, lack of respect for public morals, harm to a district’s reputation, hatred, threats, incitement, pornography, obscenity, sexual activity, and violations of intellectual property, common law, or privacy rights.

## Registration requirements

Individual:

- Personal email that is not a school email.
- Home address with 9-digit ZIP, congressional district, and member of Congress.
- School address with 9-digit ZIP, congressional district, and member of Congress.
- Parent or guardian name and email.
- Coding teacher or mentor name and email (optional).
- Personal profile and eligibility quiz.

Team: the same information for every member. One teammate creates the team profile and invites up to three others. One team enters once.

Portal: https://webportalapp.com/sp/congressional_app_challenge_2026

## Submission package

1. Working app created after October 30, 2025.
2. Public YouTube or Vimeo demonstration video, 1–3 minutes. Videos outside that range may be penalized. This is not a video-creation contest. Judges use the video to learn the app.
3. Written answers.
4. Exit questionnaire after the deadline, completed individually by every teammate. Teammates may share the link.

Video must include:

- Name of each participant
- Name of the app
- Purpose in one clear sentence
- Target audience
- Tools and coding languages used
- A showcase of functionality

The video must be set to public.

Typical written questions from the rules:

1. What is the title of your app?
2. Explain the app’s purpose.
3. What inspired you to create this app?
4. What technical/coding difficulty did you face, and how did you address it?
5. What did you learn while participating? What was your biggest takeaway?
6. What would you change in a 2.0?

Office FAQ summary of the student path: check eligibility, create an app, create a demo video, review the submission checklist, register and submit by October 26, 2026, then complete the exit questionnaire.

## Judging

Reviewed by the member of Congress and their office. They may use a panel.

Suggested criteria:

- Quality of the idea, including creativity and originality
- Implementation of the idea, including user experience and design
- Demonstrated excellence of coding and programming skills

Members may substitute or modify the process. Judging is supposed to be fair and impartial and is left to the member’s discretion. Odds depend on the number and quality of eligible submissions.

Offices are told to pick an odd number of judges with basic programming familiarity and no conflict of interest. Sample rubric: https://www.congressionalappchallenge.us/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/CAC-Judging-Rubric.pdf

One recognized winning app per hosting district.

## Prizes and recognition

Confirmed for 2026 district winners:

1. Winning app eligible to be displayed in the U.S. Capitol. The about page says each winning app may be on display for one year.
2. Winning app eligible to be featured on House.gov.
3. Invitation to #HouseOfCode in Washington, D.C.
4. Waived copyright registration application fee through the ARTS Act: https://copyright.gov/arts-act/
5. Press release on the Congressional App Challenge website.
6. Additional sponsor prizes are possible and were still TBA on the office FAQ.

No standard national cash prize.

A member’s office may give nominal awards such as certificates, folders, and frames. A local organization may donate a prize of $50 or less directly to entrants or winners. Donations over $50 from a single source must be directed to the national sponsor and made available to winners nationwide. Do not plan around a large local cash prize.

#HouseOfCode for this cycle is spring 2027 at the U.S. Capitol. Winners demo apps, meet their representative, and see the work displayed. The prior celebration (#HouseOfCode 2026) drew over 1,000 people, including dozens of members.

**Student travel and lodging are the student’s responsibility.** Official House funds cannot pay student travel.

## Rights the student gives up by entering

The student owns the intellectual property. Entering grants Congress and the administrator a royalty-free license, without limitation, to:

- Post the submissions online
- Make the winners available for the course of the competition and 1 month after the conclusion of the submission period
- Use the submissions for noncommercial purposes after the submission period

Except where prohibited, entering consents to use of the participant’s name, likeness, photograph, voice, opinions, and/or hometown and state for promotion, without further payment.

Decisions of the administrators and judges are binding. Administrators may cancel, suspend, modify, or disqualify for fraud, technical failure, tampering, rule violations, or unsportsmanlike conduct.

If a submission is lost or corrupted, the only remedy is to provide another submission.

## Agent working rules for this folder

Goal: help the student ship one eligible app and a complete submission before noon Eastern on October 26, 2026.

Do this:

- Build one original, working app. Prefer a small app that clearly runs over a large unfinished idea.
- Keep a short disclosure list of libraries, tools, and any AI use. That list goes in the submission.
- The demo video script must hit every required point in under 3 minutes.
- Do not invent a district, a member, a school, or a 9-digit ZIP. Ask for the home address and school address, then look them up.
- Confirm the district is on the participating list before spending days on a submission path that cannot be entered.
- Do not add a second app or a second team entry for the same student.
- Do not scrape or store parent emails, home addresses, or other personal data in this repo unless the student explicitly asks to keep a private notes file. Prefer telling them what to type into the portal.
- Do not promise Capitol display, House.gov placement, or travel funding. Those are “eligible” / invitation items, and travel is unpaid.
- If a local office has announced a theme, follow that theme.

Suggested build order when the student is ready:

1. Confirm district and eligibility.
2. Pick a one-sentence purpose and an audience.
3. Build the smallest version that demonstrates that purpose.
4. Record the demo against the required checklist.
5. Draft the six written answers, including the technical difficulty and the AI/library disclosure.
6. Submit in the portal before noon Eastern on October 26, 2026.
7. Complete the exit questionnaire when the link arrives.
